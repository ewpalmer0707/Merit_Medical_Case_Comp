## The flow I'm proposing

### 1. Open with the story: "The problem is real, and it already happened" (I present this)
- In 2024 a small Chinese company, Jiangsu Shenli Medical, made the syringes that went inside Merit's custom kits. The FDA sent it a warning letter in March 2024, and Merit had to recall the affected kits in June 2024 (Class II). Cardinal Health's kits that contained Merit kits were recalled too. [merit-real-suppliers.md]
- Merit couldn't see that supplier clearly, and it still hurt Merit, its customers, and potentially patients.
- That is a big problem for a company that prides itself on being sustainable and well run: three Newsweek "Greenest" awards, ISO 14001 at every site, MSCI AA (cite the case for MSCI; there's no public Merit announcement).
- Tie to Scope 3: Merit's own 2025 report names Scope 3 emissions from suppliers in China and Mexico as a top risk. You can't measure or cut what you can't see. [alisha-jerauld.md]

### 2. The landscape: what Merit is dealing with right now and why this should be a priority
- **The supplier base:** ~1,030 direct suppliers in 42 countries. The top 78 make up ~65% of spend and are mostly already reporting. The gap is small, single-plant makers in Mexico and China that can't complete EcoVadis.
- **The rollout is new:** Merit switched from Sedex (2024) to EcoVadis (2025). The stall is a first attempt, so it can still be fixed. [alisha-jerauld.md]
- **The public story is ahead of the numbers:** Scope 3 is unmeasured (2024 baseline only), Scope 1+2 is only −2% vs 2020 against a −40% goal, and Merit publishes no EcoVadis coverage numbers.
- **Outside pressure:**
  - The NHS gives at least 10% tender weight to net zero and social value, and requires a Scope 1–3 carbon plan from Apr 2027.
  - France has scored the environment in every public-hospital tender since Aug 2026.
  - The EU ban on vague claims started Sept 27 2026.
  - US GPOs (Vizient, Premier) ask suppliers for emissions data. [customer-esg-demands.md]
- **Leadership:** new CEO Martha Aronson (Oct 2025) lists "responsible supply-chain practices" as a priority.
- **Money is not the constraint:** the CGI FCF target is already met (~$401M), and the Q2 2026 margin was 22.6%.

### 3. Implementation: sort every supplier into tiers, then a tailored path to an EcoVadis score for each tier
- Step 1: score all 1,030 suppliers on sole-source/switching cost × spend × country risk × readiness → tiers. The rule is the template that scales.
- Step 2: each tier gets its own path to a third-party score:
  - **Tier 1 · Anchors** (big, already rated): accept the EcoVadis/CDP/SMETA score they already have for another customer; minimum 45, then keep improving. Nearly free.
  - **Tier 2 · Critical & stuck** (small, sole-source, Mexico first): EcoVadis Vitals (≤30 min) in year 1, then a full EcoVadis score by year 2. Support:
    - Merit pays for the invitation and subsidizes the subscription
    - Merit pre-fills the supplier's baseline from spend data
    - A named coach who speaks their language
    - IFC Mexico program partnership
    - Cheaper early payment through NAFIN
    - Longer contracts
    - "One scorecard for all your customers"
  - **Tier 3 · Long tail** (small, easy to replace): exempt below a spend threshold, short form only.
- Pilot: 10–15 Tijuana suppliers, kicking off Dec 2026. China in 2027.

### 4. Who else has done this (proof it works)
- **Medtronic:** one-on-one coaching; 34 of 116 flagged suppliers improved in a year.
- **Schneider:** pre-filled supplier baselines; top-1,000 suppliers' operational CO2 down ~56%.
- **GSK / HSBC Mexico:** buyer-paid assessments → higher response.
- **CDP/HSBC:** across 23,000 suppliers, financial incentives made them 52% more likely to cut emissions than training alone.
- **Better Work (ILO/IFC):** trained supervisors → factory lines 22% more productive.
- **Peer benchmark:** Stryker 85% of direct spend assessed by 2027.
- [analogues-buyer-programs.md, impact-measurement.md]

### 5. Financials
- **Cost:** ~$0.6M/yr ≈ 0.04% of revenue ≈ 0.2% of non-GAAP operating income.
- **Breakeven:** prevent one sole-source supplier failure every ~4 years (~$2.5M each, labeled assumption).
- **Why disruption matters:** McKinsey says supply shocks cost the average medtech company 38% of a year's earnings per decade. Baxter/Helene: ~$200M of lost sales from one plant. Merit makes ~$4.15M of revenue per day. [financials-value.md]
- **Update the night of Thu Oct 29** after Q3 earnings come out.

### 6. Close: how this lifts Merit's standards
- Map back to HEART:
  - **Health:** workers in supplier plants, devices reaching patients
  - **Excellence:** the same bar for everyone, phased in
  - **Agility:** country-by-country risk
  - **Responsibility:** a real Scope 3 baseline
  - **Teamwork:** "we listen" to suppliers first
- Merit goes from a big company asking for paperwork to a partner that helps small suppliers get there, and can honestly report it.

## Vetting notes (Claude's flags against the research, for the consolidation)
1. **Don't overstate the Shenli story.** The recall was a regulatory/quality problem (the syringes weren't FDA-cleared). It was not an emissions or eco problem. Frame it as the *visibility* problem: "the same blind spot that let an uncleared syringe into our kits also hides labor and emissions risk." Saying it hurt Merit's eco image, or that tiers would "avoid this completely," overclaims. Alisha will know the details. Better: "catch it earlier."
2. **"High impact, low effort first" needs reconciling.** By our own grid, the critical small Mexico/China suppliers are high impact but **high** effort. The low-effort wins are the Tier 1 anchors (accept existing scores). Suggested wording: "Run both at once: lock in the easy 65% of spend in month 1, and put the real effort into the critical few." That also answers the judge question "why leave 65% of spend on the table?" (pushback-checklist #1).
3. **Say "score/sort the suppliers," not "audit."** An audit means on-site visits (Merit did only 29 in 2025), and that would sound expensive and slow.
4. **Gaps in this flow vs the rubric:**
   - **Social Impact Cycle** is 25% and needs its own beat (Love the Problem → Design → Implement → Evaluate), plus unintended consequences and tripwires.
   - **What suppliers get in return** should be explicit; the case asks for it directly.
   - **What Merit says publicly** and how it avoids over-claiming (case question 5).
   - **Metrics with numbers:** spend coverage *and* critical-supplier coverage, plus a worker-pay/hours outcome.

5. **Timing:** six sections plus the missing beats in 10 minutes is tight. The current outline runs ~8:55.
