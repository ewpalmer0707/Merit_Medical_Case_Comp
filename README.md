## The case in one page (`case-materials/Merit Medical Case.txt` has the full text)
**Protagonist:** Alisha Jerauld, VP ESG. Six weeks to bring the Board's Governance & Sustainability Committee a supply-chain sustainability strategy Merit can **start piloting in 2026**.

**Company:** Merit Medical Systems (South Jordan UT; founded 1987; disposable devices for cardiology/radiology/oncology/dialysis). 2025: net sales **$1.516B**, net income **$128.5M**, ~**7,110** associates, sites in Utah, Galway, Tijuana, Singapore. Vision "most customer-focused company in healthcare"; mission Understand · Innovate · Deliver; values **HEART** = Health, Excellence (supply-chain partners held to same standards), Agility (country-by-country risk so devices keep reaching patients), Responsibility (measure footprint yearly), Teamwork ("we listen").

**Financial guardrails — Continued Growth Initiatives (3 yrs ending Dec 31 2026):** 5–7% organic CC revenue growth; **20–22% non-GAAP op margin in 2026**; **≥$400M cumulative FCF**. 2025 actual: 6.8% growth, 20.3% margin, >$215M FCF. 2026 is the final year → any spend must be small relative to a ~$300M op-income base, or self-funding.

**Supply chain (Exhibit 2):** ~**1,030 direct suppliers, 42 countries**; **top 78 = ~65% of spend**; 49% in continental US; 11% diverse-supplier spend; **29 supplier audits in 2025**; **9–12 months to (re)qualify a supplier** (FDA) → switching is slow, so hard-to-replace suppliers are a patient-safety issue (ECRI: ~half of clinicians report shortages delaying treatment).

**Problem 1 — uneven EcoVadis rollout.** Big public suppliers finished fast; small/mid precision-parts makers and contract manufacturers in **Mexico and China** (no sustainability staff, no emissions tracking, sometimes no English docs) haven't. Merit's own materiality assessment flags "management of supplier relationships" and fairness to smaller suppliers; its climate research flags Mexico and China/Singapore as highest-risk for this exact issue. Merit is legally obligated to monitor supply-chain labor (California Transparency in Supply Chains Act, UK Modern Slavery Act) → compliance, not just reputation.

**Problem 2 — telling the ESG story without overreaching.** Real awards (3× Newsweek, Gallup Exceptional Workplace, 93% survey participation) but: **Scope 3 unmeasured (2024 baseline only)**, Scope 1+2 emissions **rose 2% in 2025** (30,875 vs 22,688 MT; 2020 baseline 31,452; goal −40% by 2030), TRIR up 0.9→1.0, renewable electricity 27%. EU: CSRD narrowed/delayed (Dec 2025 omnibus); Green Claims Directive stalled but a binding law bans vague/offset-based "climate neutral" claims from **Sept 27 2026**; MDR + Batteries Regulation + PFAS/EtO scrutiny = constant compliance re-configuration whose cost lands in price. Silence also costs: hospitals screen suppliers on ESG; some customers are CSRD-covered; ESG reputation is a hiring edge.

**Exhibit 3 — Power × Readiness segmentation (illustrative):**
| | Low readiness | Moderate | High readiness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High power** (sole-source/high spend) | **Critical single-plant makers, Mexico/China** — biggest blind spot | Mid-size specialty mfrs w/ partial reporting | **"Anchor Partners"** — large, public, already reporting |
| **Low power** (fragmented/substitutable) | Small commodity-input suppliers | Regional distributors / packaging | Small suppliers already certified via other customers |

**Exhibit 5 — the two default strategies (starting points, NOT the answer):** A) uniform EcoVadis mandate, fixed deadline (fast on paper; shallow answers; strains hard-to-replace suppliers; strong on Excellence, weak on Teamwork/Agility). B) Top-78 first (cheap, high-quality data on 65% of spend; leaves the Mexico/China blind spot untouched for years; weak on Health/Responsibility).

**Four stakeholders to serve:** (1) suppliers, esp. high-power/low-readiness in Mexico & China; (2) hospital/health-system customers; (3) Board & investors (CGI targets); (4) Merit associates — procurement & quality teams.

**Central question:** What supplier sustainability strategy should Merit adopt to build credible, verifiable data across its global supply base, strengthen the supplier relationships patients depend on, and turn that into trust/competitive advantage with customers, investors, employees — reflecting HEART and fitting the financial commitments?

**Your proposal must make five things clear:** (1) who first, why, what waits; (2) what Merit asks of suppliers **and what suppliers get in return**; (3) cost, who pays, fit with margin/cash; (4) metrics, targets, timeline; (5) what Merit says publicly, to whom, and how it avoids over-claiming.
**Must also:** be a template that scales to 1,030 suppliers; improve data coverage/quality **without disrupting supply or overburdening small suppliers** (TOMS unintended-consequences example); answer (a) where first & why (Exh 2–3), (b) how it builds trust with the 4 groups incl. what info is shared with whom and when, (c) cost / return / how success is measured.
