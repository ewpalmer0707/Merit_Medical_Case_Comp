# Pushback checklist — re-run this AFTER the analogue research is in and the approach is finalized

Angle (Oct 24): risk-weighted sequencing with a supplier-first design. Start where risk and leverage are highest: small, single-plant, sole-source component makers in Mexico/China that can't complete EcoVadis but Merit cannot lose. Top 78 run as an autopilot track, not skipped.

Framing rule: say "risk-weighted sequencing," never "start with the hardest." Same idea; one sounds like strategy, the other like a gamble.

## Questions the deck must answer before it's done
1. **"Why leave 65% of spend on the table?"** Show two tracks: the 78 get a deadline (mostly rated already, cheap coverage); the critical small group gets the program (risk coverage). Spend coverage AND risk coverage.
2. **"Which suppliers exactly, and how do you know?"** A selection rule (sole-source × spend × country risk × readiness) plus a *labeled estimate* of how many land in the box. The rule is the template that scales to 1,030 — say so.
3. **"Where's the verifiable data?"** The ladder must end at a third-party scorecard/audit (EcoVadis Vitals on-ramp → full rating by year 2). Self-assessment alone fails the brief.
4. **"What do suppliers get?"** Concrete and costed: buyer-paid first assessment, Spanish/Mandarin coaching, faster payment or longer contracts for completers, one scorecard that serves all their customers. Borrow numbers from the analogue research.
5. **TOMS trap, turned on us.** Dependency, score-gaming, painting a target on suppliers for other customers' demands. Name the harm, name the tripwire, name the owner. Use Teamwork ("we listen"): ask suppliers what they need before designing help.
6. **"What does Merit say publicly in 2026 if hard data comes slowly?"** Report coverage of *critical* suppliers (not just spend) and state what Merit doesn't yet know. This is the credible-claims story for Alisha's Problem 2.
7. **Doesn't sound like charity.** Every slide ties to a device reaching a patient or a number Merit can defend. If it reads as CSR, Creativity goes up and everything else goes down.

## Also check at the end
- Pilot is startable in 2026 (Tijuana first: Merit has ~2,000 employees there; China = phase 2).
- Cost is sized against ~$300M operating income and the 20–22% margin target; who pays is explicit.
- Peer benchmark on the slide (Stryker 85% of spend by 2027 etc.) and Merit's number beats or matches it on a *risk* basis.
- Q3 2026 earnings (Oct 29) checked for anything that changes the financial framing.
- Social Impact Cycle slide uses Ballard's official stages (Love the Problem → Design → Implement → Evaluate) and lands on "socially strategic."
