## Slide 1 · Hook · Eli · 0:25
> Good morning. We're Team 23. In June 2024, Merit recalled some of its custom procedure kits. The problem was a syringe inside them, made by a Chinese company the FDA had warned months earlier. Merit only found out through one of its own suppliers. Merit couldn't see that far down its supply chain, and that blind spot is what we're here to fix.

## Slide 2 · The core problem · Eli · 0:55
> Here's the situation. Merit buys from about 1,030 suppliers in 42 countries. The top 78 make up 65% of spend, and most of them are big companies that already report. The gap is the small, single-plant makers in Mexico and China. No sustainability staff, no emissions tracking. They're also the hardest to replace: requalifying a supplier takes 9 to 12 months, and patients wait.
>
> Meanwhile, Merit's story is ahead of its numbers. Scope 3 isn't measured yet, Scope 1 and 2 are only 2% below 2020 against a 40% goal, and Merit's own report calls Scope 3 from China and Mexico a top risk.
>
> So the root problem is simple: Merit asks every supplier the same question, so the ones that matter most are the ones that can't answer it.

## Slide 3 · Why the defaults fail · Eli · 0:35
> The case gives Merit two defaults. Option A: mandate EcoVadis for everyone. That gets forms, not data. Unverified supplier surveys average 89%, with 40% perfect scores, and suppliers handed demands without help start walking away. Option B: start with the top 78. Fast and cheap, but it leaves Mexico and China dark for years. We keep B's speed and fix A's blind spot. Cameron will show you how we sort the suppliers.

## Slide 4 · Sorting suppliers into tiers · Cameron · 1:10
> Step one is sorting, and Merit already has the data in its vendor master. We score every supplier on four things: how hard they are to replace, how much Merit spends with them, country risk, and how ready they are to report. That puts each one in a tier.
>
> Tier 1 is the anchors, about 65 of the top 78. Most already have an EcoVadis score for another customer, so we ask them to share it, minimum 45. That locks in about 65% of spend in month one.
>
> Tier 2 is critical and stuck: 30 to 50 small suppliers, mostly in Mexico and China, that Merit can't lose and that can't do EcoVadis alone. That's where the real help goes.
>
> Tier 3 is the long tail, about 900 easy-to-replace suppliers. They answer a 30-minute questionnaire, and Merit estimates the rest from its own spend data.
>
> The rule is the real deliverable: it works for 1,030 suppliers today and every new one tomorrow. Nolan will show how the help works.

## Slide 5 · How the data gets collected · Nolan · 1:05
> So how do you get credible data out of a 40-person machine shop in Tijuana? Most of it already exists. In Mexico every invoice is a digital CFDI file, including the electricity bill. In China it's the e-fapiao. And most small manufacturers keep their books in software like CONTPAQi or Kingdee.
>
> So Merit builds an AI evidence assistant that reads those records and organizes what EcoVadis and our Scope 3 math need: kilowatt-hours, fuel, policies, and a source for every number.
>
> The obvious question is trust. Why let Merit's software into your books? You don't have to. Merit pays a local consultant who already knows the supplier's software. The consultant runs the assistant on-site, the raw data never leaves the building, and the supplier approves the evidence pack before anything is shared.
>
> One honest limit: the tool only finds records that exist. If something was never measured, that gap becomes a plan to start measuring it.

## Slide 6 · Tijuana pilot and timeline · Nolan · 0:40
> We start in Tijuana, next to Merit's largest plant. This November and December, the Board approves, we sort all 1,030 suppliers, and we test the assistant with two suppliers. Listening sessions and readiness requests go to 12 pilot suppliers in December, so the pilot starts in 2026. In early 2027 they produce their first evidence packs. Then there's a gate: we only expand if cost per pack and supplier retention hit their targets. China follows in late 2027, and by 2028 the rule covers all 42 countries. Eli will cover whether Merit can afford it.

## Slide 7 · Is this actually feasible? · Eli · 0:55
> So, is this actually feasible? Year one costs about $810,000, capped. That includes about $150,000 to build the assistant once and reuse it for every supplier. After that it's about $660,000 a year, roughly a quarter of one percent of Merit's non-GAAP operating income. Merit is already inside its 20 to 22% margin target and has already hit its $400 million cash-flow goal.
>
> What does Merit get back? McKinsey found supply shocks cost the average medtech company 38% of a year's earnings every decade. If losing one critical supplier costs about $2.5 million, which is our estimate, the program pays for itself by catching one failure early every four years. We're not promising to prevent recalls. We're giving Merit the visibility to catch them sooner. Cameron will show why suppliers want in.

## Slide 8 · What suppliers get · Cameron · 0:45
> Picture running a small precision shop in China. Your team is focused on good parts, shipped on time, and now Merit wants sustainability evidence. Why bother? Merit pays for the assessment. Suppliers that finish get paid faster through NAFIN's fee-free early-payment program, and qualify for longer contracts. One EcoVadis score works for all their customers, so fewer audits. We help Mexican suppliers handle the new 40-hour week without cutting pay. And the score helps them win other buyers. CDP found suppliers are 52% more likely to cut emissions when buyers offer financial incentives, not just training. Nolan will show how we know it's working.

## Slide 9 · What success looks like · Nolan · 0:40
> Peers like Stryker measure spend: 85% of direct spend assessed by 2027. We'll match that, but we also track what matters to patients: critical suppliers with a traceable evidence pack, 60% in 2027 and 90% in 2028. No peer reports that. Tier 2 retention stays at 100%, because losing a critical supplier means we failed. For workers, we track legal hours with pay held, against a wait-listed comparison group. Backorders caused by suppliers go to zero. And publicly, Merit reports only what it can prove: coverage, and how much of Scope 3 is real supplier data.

## Slide 10 · Others have done it · Nolan · 0:30
> And this has worked before. Schneider Electric helped more than 700 suppliers measure their footprint for the first time. Philips partnered with a Chinese supplier, Zhejiang Haishun, instead of auditing it, and found over 120 improvements. CDP and HSBC studied 23,000 suppliers and found financial incentives beat training alone. And Better Work found factory lines got 22% more productive after supervisor training. Cameron will bring it home.

## Slide 11 · Social Impact Cycle + tripwires · Cameron · 0:50
> We built this on the Social Impact Cycle. We loved the problem: small plants asked for something new, with no help, while a labor reform lands on them. So we listen first, design the sort and the matched help, implement with the Tijuana 12, and evaluate outputs, outcomes and impact. Good intentions can backfire, so every risk has a tripwire. Help is time-boxed so no one depends on it. A third party reviews evidence so paperwork can't replace practice. Retention has to stay at 100%. Weekly pay has to hold. And supplier data stays with the supplier. That makes this socially strategic: healthy suppliers are core to Merit's business.

## Slide 12 · Mission, vision, values + the ask · Cameron · 0:40
> Merit wants to be the most customer-focused company in healthcare, and that customer is a patient waiting on a device. Its mission is Understand, Innovate, Deliver: we understand suppliers by asking what's blocking them, we innovate with an assistant that keeps their data in their hands, and we deliver by keeping critical parts flowing. That's HEART carried down the supply chain. We ask the Board for three things: approve the sorting rule, fund about $810,000 for year one, and start in Tijuana this December. The next Shenli gets caught earlier. Thank you.
