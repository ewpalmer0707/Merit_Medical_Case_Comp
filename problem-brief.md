# The Problem — Merit Medical case (master this before ideating)

## One sentence
Merit is collecting sustainability awards faster than it is collecting sustainability *facts*, and the missing facts sit with the suppliers it can least afford to lose and least able to answer.

## The setup
- Merit makes disposable devices used inside the body (guidewires, catheters, dialysis catheters). Hospitals reorder constantly. A missing part at one supplier = a missing device in a procedure room. ECRI: ~half of clinicians say shortages have delayed treatment.
- Merit buys from ~1,030 direct suppliers in 42 countries. The top 78 = ~65% of spend. Only 49% are in the continental US. Replacing a supplier takes 9–12 months (FDA requalification), so Merit cannot just walk away from a bad or non-responsive one.
- Historically Merit picked suppliers on quality alone. Environmental/social criteria are new. 29 supplier audits in all of 2025, for 1,030 suppliers.
- Merit tried to fix this by sending suppliers to EcoVadis (a third-party scorecard). Most suppliers have NOT completed it.

## Problem 1 — the data gap is in exactly the wrong place
Who finished EcoVadis: big, often public suppliers who already report to other customers. Easy, and they're mostly not where the risk is.
Who didn't: small and mid-size precision-parts makers and contract manufacturers, especially in Mexico and China. They have no sustainability staff, no emissions-tracking system, sometimes no English documentation of things they may already do fine. To them the EcoVadis request looks like unfunded homework from a customer far bigger than they are.

Why that matters, in layers:
1. **Risk**: Merit's own climate research says Mexico and China/Singapore are the highest-risk regions for this issue because local rules pushing better reporting are weak there. So the blind spot is darkest where the risk is highest.
2. **Continuity**: many of these are sole-source, single-plant, high-switching-cost suppliers (Exhibit 3's top-left box). Push too hard and they quietly drop Merit or give shallow answers. Either way patients lose.
3. **Compliance**: Merit is legally required to monitor labor practices in its chain (California Transparency in Supply Chains Act, UK Modern Slavery Act). Not knowing is not a defense.
4. **Scope 3**: Merit cannot measure its supplier emissions (which dwarf its own) without supplier data. It has only set a 2024 baseline. Its "Responsibility" value is "measure the footprint year after year", and right now it can't.
5. **Fairness**: Merit's own materiality assessment flagged "how fairly Merit manages its size advantage over smaller suppliers". A blunt mandate is the exact behavior it said it would watch for.

## Problem 2 — the public story is ahead of the numbers
Real wins: three Newsweek recognitions in a year, Gallup Exceptional Workplace, 93% survey participation.
Hard numbers underneath: Scope 3 unmeasured; Scope 1+2 emissions are only **−2% vs the 2020 baseline** (30,875 vs 31,452 MT) against a **−40% by 2030** goal. The 2024 figure (22,688) excluded refrigerants, so the apparent 2025 jump is an accounting change, not a real spike; the case text's "rose 2%" is loose wording. Scope 1 is actually +12% vs 2020; Scope 2 −9%; intensity per $ sales −38% (growth did the work). Renewable electricity 27%; TRIR up 0.9→1.0; no SBTi validation. Merit's own report: "more must be done to move the needle." (Research §1, Sustainability Report p.15–17.)
The rules on talking about this are tightening: an EU law bans vague or offset-based "climate neutral" claims from Sept 27 2026; FTC Green Guides govern US claims; CSRD was narrowed but hospital customers covered by it still ask suppliers for data. Plus MDR, Batteries Regulation, PFAS and EtO scrutiny mean EU compliance is a moving target whose cost shows up in price.
But silence is not free: hospital systems screen suppliers on ESG, some customers must report their own supply chains, and Merit's employer reputation is a real recruiting edge.
So the question is not "say more or say less". It's: how do you say only what the data supports, while building the data so you can say more next year.

## The box Merit has to fit inside
- **Money**: 2026 is the last year of the Continued Growth Initiatives: 5–7% growth, 20–22% non-GAAP operating margin, $400M cumulative free cash flow. 2025 landed at 6.8% / 20.3% / >$215M. On ~$1.5B sales, every $15M of cost is a full margin point. The plan has to be cheap, self-funding, or clearly pay back.
- **Time**: Alisha has six weeks to get Board committee approval and must start a pilot in 2026.
- **Scale**: whatever works for the first suppliers has to be a template for all 1,030 in 42 countries.
- **Do no harm**: don't push out small suppliers, don't disrupt supply, don't over-claim (the TOMS lesson: good intentions, bad side effects).
- **Values**: HEART. Health (workers across the whole value chain), Excellence (partners held to Merit's standards), Agility (country-by-country risk so devices keep flowing), Responsibility (measure yearly), Teamwork ("we listen", suppliers should be heard not just surveyed).

## Why the two obvious answers fail
- **A. Mandate everyone by a deadline**: fast on paper, but the small suppliers either can't comply or fake it. Strains the relationships Merit can't replace. Strong on Excellence, violates Teamwork and Agility.
- **B. Top 78 first**: cheap, gets quality data on 65% of spend quickly. But it leaves the Mexico/China single-plant suppliers (the actual blind spot) untouched for years. Covers spend, not risk. Weak on Health and Responsibility.
Note: spend coverage ≠ risk coverage. The judges want you to notice that.

## The core tension (hold this in your head)
Merit's size is both the tool and the danger. It is big enough to force compliance and big enough to crush a 40-person plant in Tijuana by trying. The winning idea makes small suppliers *want* to report, by giving them something back, and sequences by risk plus leverage rather than by spend alone.

## Who has to be satisfied, and what each one wants
| Stakeholder | What they want | What they fear |
|---|---|---|
| Small high-power suppliers (MX/CN) | Keep the business; low burden; help, in their language; a reason it benefits them | Unfunded homework; being dropped; audits as punishment |
| Hospital / health-system customers | Credible ESG data to pass through their own screens and CSRD reports | Supply disruption; greenwashing exposure |
| Board & investors | Credible, defensible program that doesn't dent the 2026 margin/FCF targets | Reputational blow-up; big new cost center |
| Merit associates (procurement, quality) | A workable process with clear owners that doesn't double their workload | Another compliance layer on top of MDR etc. |

## What "credible, verifiable" actually means
Third-party validated (EcoVadis score, audit, certification), covering a meaningful share of spend AND of risk, with a baseline and a trend, and with Merit able to act on it (corrective actions, requalification, sourcing decisions). A pile of self-reported survey answers is not that.

## The five things the proposal must answer (verbatim from the case)
1. Who Merit engages first, why, and what waits.
2. What Merit asks of suppliers, and what suppliers get in return.
3. What it costs, who pays, how it fits margin and cash targets.
4. How Merit knows it's working: metrics, targets, timeline.
5. What Merit says publicly, to whom, and how it avoids claiming more than its data supports.

## Answers from the outside research (Oct 24; see `research/outside-research.md`)
- **FCF target is already met**: 2024 + 2025 FCF = ~$401M vs the $400M CGI target. Q2 2026 non-GAAP op margin was 22.6% and guidance was raised. So the real financial constraint is *margin discipline and credibility*, not cash. (Q3 2026 earnings land **Oct 29**, the day before we present — check them.)
- **Merit publishes NO EcoVadis numbers** (no coverage %, no target). The "stalling in Mexico/China" story is case-packet only. Its report just says it is "partnering with EcoVadis to design and implement a new platform." We should propose the coverage target Merit doesn't have.
- **EcoVadis cost to a supplier**: no official price list; third-party figures ~€1,080 Basic to ~€7,650 Corporate per year (US ~$500–11,000+). Buyer side ~$15–40K/yr for 50–100 suppliers. 12-month validity; scores 0–100 (Good = 45–64); "absence of evidence = absence of management" (why small suppliers score badly). A light "Vitals" questionnaire takes ≤30 min. Repeat assessments improve ~+15 points on average.
- **Peer benchmarks**: Stryker 85% of direct spend assessed by 2027; BD 85% of prioritized suppliers assessed / 90% of eligible spend goal; Abbott >90% of direct spend screened but admits its supplier science-based-target goal (82% by 2026) is "unlikely to be met" (43%); J&J 37% vs 80% by 2028; Philips 46% vs 50% by 2025 and cites **preferential payment terms** as a supplier incentive. Most peer "support" is just webinars and guides, so real capability-building is open space.
- **Customer pull is real and dated**: NHS requires Scope 1/2/3 Carbon Reduction Plans from all suppliers from **April 2027**; Vizient asks suppliers for Scope 1 & 2; medtech supply chain ≈ 7% of US health-sector emissions (CHARME, Medtronic on its exec committee).
- **"Something in return" models with numbers**: IFC Global Trade Supplier Finance $22B to >2,500 suppliers, 84% sustainability-linked; Schneider Zero Carbon Project cut top-1,000 suppliers' operational CO2 ~56%; Walmart–HSBC tiered financing; **IFC Mexico Supplier Development Program (Aug 2025 MoU, ~400 SMEs)** is the closest Mexico hook.
- **Materiality wording to quote**: "Management of Supplier Relationships, Including Payment Practices" = managing "the size disparity between its smaller suppliers that could potentially lead to supplier exploitation."
- **Ballard Social Impact Cycle (official)**: core "love the one" + Love the Problem → Design the Change → Implement → Evaluate, with outputs/outcomes/impact and root-cause language from the guidebook. The six-stage "journey" slide is only in the kickoff deck; cite the case packet for it.
- **Greenwashing rules**: EU EmpCo from Sept 27 2026 bans generic claims, offset-based neutrality, uncertified labels; FTC Green Guides still the 2012 version. CSRD Omnibus value-chain cap: suppliers ≤1,000 employees can refuse data beyond the VSME standard → supports a "proportionate ask" for small suppliers.
- **Why-it-matters data**: FDA shortage list (Jun 2026) includes angiographic injectors/syringes, a Merit category; Premier: shortages cost a 650-bed system ~$3.5M/yr; Hurricane Helene/Baxter: 60% of US IV fluids from one plant.
- **Traps**: Merit's report says 42 supplier audits in one place and 29 in the SASB index; "direct suppliers" count changed from 5,621 (2024) to 1,030 (2025) by definition; the $150–400K / 6–18-month supplier-switch cost is a single consultancy estimate; the BD and Schneider peer figures rest on secondary summaries, so open the primary reports before putting them on a slide.

## Questions we should be able to answer before picking a strategy
- Why would a 40-person supplier in Tijuana or Dongguan do this? What's in it for them?
- How many suppliers are in the "high power, low readiness" box, and what share of spend? (Not in the case; estimate and label it.)
- What does EcoVadis cost a supplier, and who pays?
- What do peers (Medtronic, BD, Boston Sci, Stryker) already do, and what % of suppliers by spend do they cover? Judges score benchmarks.
- What can Merit honestly say publicly in 2026 vs 2027?
- What is the first thing that goes wrong, and who owns the fix?
