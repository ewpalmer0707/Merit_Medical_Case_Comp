# Strategy recommendation — what the companies that succeeded actually did (Oct 24)

## The verdict on our angle
Nobody who succeeded "started with the hardest group." Everyone who succeeded **tiered the whole base by readiness and put the intensive help only on the critical-but-stuck**. The successful shape is a three-tier "meet suppliers where they are" program (Medtronic's own words for its Oct 2025 program; Philips' four maturity buckets; Schneider's Aspiring/Advanced/Leaders; AstraZeneca's spend thresholds). Our instinct about *where the effort and money go* is right. Our *framing* ("start with the hardest") is wrong, and the judges will punish it on Strategic Fit and Quant Rigor. Pitch the tiered program; the critical-stuck tier is where the story, the social impact, and the innovation live.

## The eight things that worked, every time (from 18 buyer cases + Mexico/China evidence)
1. **Tier the ask by maturity/spend and say so publicly.** Light form for the tail; deadline for the big ones; intensive help only for critical-stuck.
2. **Pre-fill the first number.** Schneider estimated supplier baselines from spend so suppliers corrected instead of created (75% of its top 1,000 had never measured emissions).
3. **Buyer pays for the tool, supplier pays a token.** GSK sponsored 200 platform seats; HSBC Mexico: partial subsidy "generates higher response rates." EcoVadis invitation is free when Merit sends it.
4. **Train in the supplier's language and time zone.** Schneider 8×3-hour multilingual modules; PSCI Mandarin webinars; UN Global Compact Spain free Spanish course (5,200 SMEs).
5. **One shared assessment instead of ten.** Accept a supplier's existing EcoVadis/SMETA/SAQ from any customer. A typical small factory already gets 3–5 audits a year and pays $5–20k for them (WRAP 2023).
6. **Make the carrot financial and near-term.** CDP/HSBC 2024, 23,000 suppliers: financial incentives make suppliers **52% more likely** to cut emissions than training alone. Walmart/HSBC: cheaper early payment by score. Herdez/BBVA: preferential factoring in Mexico.
7. **Recognition ladders, not pass/fail.** AstraZeneca minimum 45 with "progressive improvement"; GM phased minimum 50 and the average landed at 52.
8. **Send a person.** Medtronic one-on-ones with sample policies: 34 of 116 flagged suppliers improved in a year. Philips engineers found 120+ fixes at one Chinese plant.

## The four things that failed, every time
- **Audit-only compliance** (Philips abandoned it; monitor-only keeps lower tiers passive).
- **Unverified yes/no questionnaires** (23,000 responses averaged 89% with 40% perfect scores; 250+ suppliers accidentally "confessed" to forced labor). Fake data.
- **Mandate without support** → attrition. 56% of large Finnish firms already swapped suppliers over ESG; 77% of UK SME manufacturers get ESG demands, ~27% get help; 84% of SMEs offered no incentive.
- **Expecting fast conversion.** ~40% finish a free course; ~18% of coached suppliers set a science-based target after years; Schneider had 10% cutting 5% after year one. Plan multi-year, partial uptake, honest numbers.

## Recommended program: "Merit Supplier Ladder" (working name) — three tiers, one scorecard, a person, and a carrot
**Selection rule (the template that scales to 1,030 in 42 countries):** score every supplier on sole-source/switching cost × spend × country risk (Merit's own TCFD: Mexico High, China/Singapore) × readiness (has a scorecard from any customer? sustainability staff? reporting history?). The rule, not the list, is the deliverable.

| Tier | Who (est., label as assumption) | What Merit asks | What suppliers get | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Anchors** | ~60–70 of the top 78; large, public, already rated | Full EcoVadis (or accept existing EcoVadis/CDP/SMETA from another customer); min score 45, progressive improvement; CDP Supply Chain only for the 10–20 largest | Nothing new needed; recognition; preferred-supplier status | Every 2–3 yrs by risk |
| **2 · Critical & stuck** (the story) | ~30–60: high-power, low-readiness, single-plant, Mexico/China first | Vitals light questionnaire (≤30 min, 14 languages) in year 1 → full EcoVadis by year 2; corrective-action plan on the platform | Merit-paid invitation + subsidized subscription; pre-filled baseline from spend; a named Merit coach (Medtronic model) + in-language training (Mexico via **IFC–INA–SE Supplier Development Program**, free, anchor-firm-led, NAFIN/Bancomext finance attached; China via EcoVadis CN platform + IPE Blue Map, **not RBA**); financial carrot: ESG-tiered early payment / reverse factoring on NAFIN Cadenas Productivas; longer contract terms for completers; "one scorecard serves all your customers" | Yearly |
| **3 · Long tail** | ~900 low-power, substitutable, mostly < $250k spend | Exempt from EcoVadis below a spend threshold (AstraZeneca model); 10-KPI short form or Vitals; China suppliers screened free on IPE Blue Map | Free, fast, in-language; no fee | Every 3 yrs |

**Pilot (2026 start, Tijuana first):** Merit has ~2,000 employees and a plant there; 73 device companies in the cluster; no competitor publicly engages Tijuana sub-suppliers on ESG → first mover. 10–15 sole-source suppliers within driving distance of the Tijuana plant. Named owner: a supplier-sustainability coordinator inside Procurement reporting to Alisha's Sustainability Council (HBR: goals without a coordinator fail). Partners: IFC PDP (ask IFC Mexico to open a medical-device chapter under its "replicate in other strategic sectors" clause), Cluster de Productos Médicos de las Californias / INDEX for workshops, NAFIN for factoring, FIDE Eco-Crédito for retrofits. China = phase 2 (2027), using IPE screening now and EcoVadis CN + consultant hours later.

**Rough cost (label as estimate):** 50 Tier-2 suppliers × (~$1,500 subscription + ~$3,000 coaching/consultant hours) ≈ $225k + 2 FTE (~$250k) + training/partner costs (~$100k) ≈ **$0.5–0.7M/yr**, i.e. ~0.04 of a margin point on ~$1.5B sales. Financing carrot costs Merit basis points on early-paid invoices, not capex. Compare: one supplier switch costs $150–400k and 6–18 months (single consultancy estimate, flag it), and shortages cost a 650-bed hospital ~$3.5M/yr.

**Targets (numeric, honest):** Year 1: 100% of Tier-2 pilot suppliers invited, ≥70% complete Vitals (Bosch got 71% on an SME form), ≥40% complete coaching (UNGC Spain rate). Year 2: ≥60% of Tier-2 hold a full EcoVadis score; median score ≥45; Scope 3 Category 1 coverage reported for X% of spend **and** Y% of critical suppliers. 2027: match Stryker's 85%-of-direct-spend-assessed benchmark, plus a *risk-coverage* number no peer publishes.

**What Merit says publicly (Problem 2):** report coverage two ways, by spend and by critical suppliers; publish what is not yet known (Scope 3 baseline 2024, in progress); no "climate neutral"/generic claims (EU EmpCo Sept 27 2026); the supplier story told as *partnership*, which is also the recruiting story.

**Social Impact Cycle mapping:** Love the Problem = small plants asked for the first time, 3–5 audits/yr, no help; Design = tiered ladder + carrot + person; Implement = Tijuana pilot via IFC PDP; Evaluate = completion, score, retention, worker outcomes; lands on **socially strategic** (continuity + Scope 3 are core business).

**Unintended consequences to name:** dependency on Merit's coach → time-boxed coaching; score-gaming → verified scorecard not self-assessment; Merit's help making suppliers visible to other buyers' demands → "one scorecard, all customers" turns that into a benefit; labor-hour reform (48→40 hrs by 2030) is the real 2026 shock for small Mexican plants → include labor-compliance help so the program solves *their* problem, not just Merit's.

## Why this wins on the rubric
- Strategic fit 25%: tiered by risk *and* spend; HEART mapped (Teamwork "we listen" = ask suppliers first; Agility = country risk; Health = workers in supplier plants; Responsibility = Scope 3 baseline; Excellence = same bar, phased).
- Social impact 25%: real suppliers, real workers, measurable, with the harm case (attrition, fake data) answered.
- Quant rigor 15%: every number above has a source; cost sized vs margin; peer benchmark on the slide.
- Feasibility 15%: Tijuana, 2026, named owner, existing partner vehicle, China deliberately phased with a legal reason (RBA listing).
- Creativity 10%: "one scorecard for all your customers," the IFC chapter, risk-coverage as a public metric, the labor-reform hook.
