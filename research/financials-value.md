### 1.3 Cost and time to requalify a sole-source supplier (improving on our one consultancy figure)
- **Still the only dollar estimate found:** $150k–$400k per component change for a mid-complexity Class II device, with **6–18 months** of internal requalification. https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/medical-device-dual-sourcing-tariff ; companion piece https://ventureoutsource.com/contract-manufacturing/fda-requalification-tariff-lock-in/. **I found no AdvaMed, McKinsey, Deloitte or academic study with a requalification dollar figure [NOT FOUND].** The AdvaMed resilience white paper (2023) has no numbers on this https://www.advamed.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Building-Supply-Chain-Resilience-White-Paper-final.pdf.
- **Supporting evidence for the time, with sources that differ from the consultancy:**
  - Merit's own 10-K: "If one of our suppliers fails to maintain compliance with our quality requirements, **we may have to qualify a new supplier and could experience manufacturing delays** as a result." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/856982/000085698226000008/mmsi-20251231x10k.htm
  - A medical-device practitioner on Gartner Peer Community targets about **12 months** of notice for end-of-life changes with sole-sourced suppliers "because of extensive validation". Their contracts require 90 days' notice for process changes and 180 days for design changes. https://www.gartner.com/peer-community/post/typical-lead-time-supplier-change-notifications-we-have-90-days-process-design-changes-180-days-eol-our-contracts-we-medical **[practitioner forum, page blocked to fetch; from search snippet]**
  - A supplier swap can require design verification, process validation and supplier qualification even when no new 510(k) is needed. https://www.mddionline.com/product-development/strategies-for-evaluating-and-validating-supplier-formulation-and-process-changes
- **Hard regulatory costs if a new 510(k) is triggered:**
  - The FDA 510(k) user fee for FY2026 was **$26,067** standard and $6,517 for small businesses. https://www.fda.gov/medical-device-user-fee-amendments-mdufa ; https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-unveils-fy-2026-user-fee-rates.html. FY2027 (current) is reported at $28,653 **[UNVERIFIED]** https://www.pureglobal.com/news/fda-fy-2027-medical-device-user-fees
  - The MDUFA V shared goal for average total time to decision on a 510(k) is **112 calendar days** for FY2025 submissions. An aggregator reports an actual average of about 146 days in 2025 **[UNVERIFIED]**. https://www.fda.gov/media/191127/download ; https://www.510kdatabase.net/510k-review-time/
  - **Takeaway for the slide:** the fee is trivial. The cost is the **months** of validation plus the review clock, during which only safety stock covers you.
- **Merit's own buffer:** Merit holds 2–7 weeks of finished-goods inventory regionally and keeps safety stock on critical raw materials (2025 Sustainability Report p.35, already in outside-research.md §1.4). **Implication [my calc]:** a requalification of 6–18 months is roughly 4–30 times longer than the inventory cover.

### 3.2 Supply-chain finance (the carrot)
- **Typical spreads:**
  - An ESG-compliant supplier saves on average **~25 bps** in sustainability-linked SCF programs. BMO: https://capitalmarkets.bmo.com/en/insights/trade-finance-tackling-sustainability-one-company-time **[from search snippet; page blocked]**

  - Santander/Sonae prices off **EcoVadis scores** (https://www.santander.com/en/press-room/press-releases/2022/05/santander-launches-first-sustainability-linked-supply-chain-finance-in-portugal).
  - Bridgestone/J.P. Morgan uses tiered pricing tied to the supplier's ESG scorecard (https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/treasury/trade-working-capital/supply-chain-esg-solution).
- **Siemens Energy (US and Mexico suppliers, Orbian platform):** a search snippet showed SOFR + 2.00% for suppliers with >$500k volume, +2.25% for $300–500k, and +3.00% for <$300k. https://siemens-energy.com/global/en/home/supplier/collaborating-with-siemens-energy/supply-chain-finance.html. **[UNVERIFIED: the live page no longer shows the grid.]**
- **Mexico benchmarks:**
  - The 28-day TIIE was **6.7559%** on Oct 19 2026 (DOF) https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5801558.
  - The average MiPyME bank lending rate was **14.40% (Aug 2025)**, down from 16.10% in 2024 (CNBV via ABM) https://www.abm.org.mx/sala-de-prensa/historico/Presentacion_29_10_2025.pdf. Banxico's reference rate was 7.75% then, so the **SME spread is about 665 bps [my calc]**.
- **NAFIN Cadenas Productivas:**
  - Electronic reverse factoring with "**tasas competitivas y sin comisión**." Affiliation is free and 100% digital.
  - The rate is set by the **anchor buyer's (EPO's) credit quality**, not the supplier's.
  - Official documents: https://www.nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/modulo-de-atencion-virtual/pdf/factoraje/cadenas_productivas.pdf ; https://nafin.com/portalnf/files/secciones/cadenas-productivas/mujerEs-factoraje/MujerEs-Factoraje-Nafin.pdf. Explainer: https://www.americanindustrialmagazine.com/blogs/finanzas/factoraje-financiero-para-pymes-mexico-2026-guia-completa.
  - Specific spread over TIIE **[NOT FOUND]**. It is published per program; ask NAFIN (800 NAFINSA).
- **What this means for Merit's cost [my calc + assumption]:**
  - In standard reverse factoring the **bank funds** the early payment, so Merit's cost is ~0. Merit could even extend its own payment terms, which is a working-capital *gain*.
  - If Merit chooses to *subsidize* an extra 50 bps for Tier-2 completers on $50M of annual spend [assumption: 50 suppliers × $1M] paid 60 days early: 0.5% × $50M × 60/365 ≈ **$41k/yr**.
  - Supplier benefit: borrowing at about TIIE + 2–3% (~9–10%) instead of 14.4% saves about 450–550 bps. On a $1M/yr customer whose invoices are prepaid 60 days, that is about **$7–9k/yr per supplier [my calc]**, enough to pay for its own EcoVadis subscription several times over.

### 5.3 Cross-check: Merit's expected disruption loss (top-down)
- McKinsey puts the 10-year cost of shocks for medtech at 38% of one year's earnings, which is **3.8% of earnings per year**.
- On FY2025 non-GAAP operating income of $307.7M, that is an **expected loss of about $11.7M/yr**. On GAAP operating income of $184.7M it is about $7.0M/yr [my calc].
- **Break-even: the Supplier Ladder must cut Merit's expected disruption losses by only about 5% (non-GAAP basis) to 9% (GAAP basis).** It targets exactly the high-power, low-readiness, single-plant suppliers where the risk concentrates.
