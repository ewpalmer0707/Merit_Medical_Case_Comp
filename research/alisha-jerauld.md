# Alisha Jerauld & Merit's official sustainability framing

Researched Oct 27 2026 for the Oct 30 BYU Business for Good case comp. Alisha Jerauld is Merit's VP of Environment, Social & Governance (ESG) and chairs the Corporate Sustainability Council.

## Bottom line first

- **She has almost no public speaking record online.** There are no interviews, podcasts, conference talks, panels, news quotes, or Ballard Center / BYU appearances that can be found. Her public voice is (a) a short undated letter on merit.com, (b) a 2020 sustainability-report page she signed, and (c) a few LinkedIn posts that show up only as search snippets (LinkedIn blocks fetching). **Most of what she "says" publicly is Merit's report language, which her team writes.** So "speaking her language" means speaking the sustainability report's language.
- **The CEO changed.** Fred Lampropoulos stepped down as CEO on **Oct 3 2025** and stays on as Chairman. **Martha G. Aronson** (ex-Ecolab, Hill-Rom, Medtronic) is now President & CEO and signs the **2025** report letter. Don't call Fred the CEO in the deck.
- **The supplier platform changed: Sedex in the 2024 report, EcoVadis in the 2025 report.** The wording is otherwise almost identical. The low completion rate in the case is therefore a **new EcoVadis rollout**, not a mature program.
- **The Supplier Code of Conduct webpage says "Last Updated: April, 2018."** Its environmental clause is one vague sentence. This is an easy, credible fix to recommend.
- **No public MSCI AA announcement from Merit was found.** It is in the case, so cite the case and not the web. Merit's blog says it reached **ISS ESG "Prime"** status (the page wouldn't load, so that is from a search snippet). Sustainalytics rates Merit's ESG risk at **15.9, Low Risk** (Jul 2024 data).

**Key supplier numbers (2025 report p.35–37):** more than 1,034 direct suppliers; **the top 78 make up more than 65% of spend**; 49% are in the continental US; 11% of spend goes to suppliers with diversity designations; 42 supplier audits and 92 SCARs (supplier corrective action requests) in 2025; less than 10% of suppliers supply 3TG (tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold) conflict minerals. **Scope 3 is still being calculated, with a 2024 baseline.** Climate goals: −40% absolute Scope 1+2 by 2030 and −65% by 2050, against a 2020 baseline (2025 result: −2%).

## Themes she emphasizes

1. **ESG as business value and risk management, not PR.** Her signature line, repeated since 2020, is "adding business value by assessing the risks and opportunities." She came up through the ERM / internal-audit side: an internal-auditor LinkedIn profile under her name is probably her, but that's unconfirmed.
2. **Transparency and rigor in reporting.** Merit reports to GRI, SASB, and TCFD, follows the GHG Protocol, rebaselined its numbers, and admits that "more must be done." The organization is self-aware about credibility, so the case's "does the story match the substance" question is hers.
3. **People first.** "People are Merit's greatest asset" and safety as the top priority. Teamwork and agility are "the heart" of sustainability.
4. **Customers and partners have their own goals.** Customers, supply-chain partners, and investors are "striving to achieve their own sustainability goals." Net-zero requirements from customers are listed as a risk.
5. **Suppliers: quality-first culture now adding E&S.** Merit officially says it historically put "quality above all else" and now needs "greater consideration of environmental and social factors." It wants to drive "our value system throughout our supply chain." It also names the size gap with small suppliers and the "underdeveloped" rules in China and Mexico as risks.
6. **Values-led governance.** HEART / The Merit Way, with "specific measures and accountability up to the highest levels." There is a Board G&S Committee and a Council she chairs.

## Phrases to echo in our deck

- "Adding business value by assessing risks and opportunities"
- "Drive our value system throughout our supply chain" (this is their own wording for the goal)
- "Greater consideration of environmental and social factors" alongside quality, which is quality-first plus E&S rather than instead of it
- "Trusted, long-term partner to customers" (CEO Aronson's framing)
- "Responsible supply-chain practices" (on the CEO's priority list for 2026+)
- "Transparency and rigor" / "measure and demonstrate progress" / "catalyst for real change"
- "More must be done to move the needle" (use carefully: it shows we read the report)
- "Manage the size disparity … ethically and responsibly" for the small-supplier support idea
- "There is a patient at the end of every decision" (HEART: Responsibility)
- "Specific measures and accountability up to the highest levels" for the KPI and governance slide
- Use **"supply chain partners"**, not "vendors"

## Likely Q&A questions she'd ask (our inference, not her words)

1. "We just moved from Sedex to EcoVadis. How does your plan raise completion among small Mexico/China suppliers without paying for their assessments ourselves?"
2. "What does this cost in 2026, and how does it fit our 20–22% margin and free-cash-flow commitments?"
3. "How does this help me defend our Newsweek and MSCI recognitions? What would a skeptic or a hospital customer see that proves substance?"
4. "Which suppliers first? Our top 78 suppliers are 65% of spend. Is your pilot spend-based, risk-based, or both?"
5. "How do we avoid exploiting the size gap with small suppliers, which is a material topic for us, while still holding them to the Supplier Code?"
6. "What happens when a critical, single-source supplier fails the assessment? Patients still need devices." (Agility, supply continuity)
7. "How does this feed our Scope 3 baseline (2024), and what will we disclose under GRI/SASB/TCFD?"
8. "What KPIs would I report to the Governance & Sustainability Committee each quarter?"
9. "Our Supplier Code was last updated in 2018. What should change?"
10. "How do Procurement and Quality, who own supplier audits and SCARs today, take this on without a new bureaucracy?"
