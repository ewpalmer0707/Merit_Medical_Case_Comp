# Supplier reporting support: proposal for team discussion

Updated October 27, 2026. This records the direction developed in this discussion; it is not an approved Merit program or a replacement for teammates' proposals.

## Recommendation

**Assess all suppliers, accept suitable existing evidence, and fund individual assistance only where a diagnosed reporting barrier justifies it.**

The regional kickoff and group training program have been dropped. The objective is credible supplier information for Merit's Scope 3 accounting while helping small suppliers build reporting capability without unnecessary burden.

## Facts, hypotheses, and boundaries

- **Case facts:** approximately 1,030 direct suppliers; top 78 account for about 65% of spending. See the [case](../case-materials/Merit%20Medical%20Case.txt).
- **Hypothesis:** perhaps 85% of the top 78 can provide information with limited assistance. No supplier-level evidence establishes this percentage.
- The top 78 are the highest-spend suppliers; do not assume every one is a large company or reporting-ready.
- Only a few suppliers may need intensive help, but this must be tested. Do not set the support budget as if this were already known.
- This is the emissions-reporting workstream. Carbon data alone does not resolve the case's broader labor, ethics, supplier-relationship, and communication issues.

## 1. Diagnose before allocating support

Use Merit's existing supplier records and a short request asking:

1. Which existing assessments, emissions inventories, or relevant disclosures can you share?
2. Which production sites supply Merit, and for what reporting period?
3. Who owns the information?
4. Which records already exist, and what specific barrier prevents a response?
5. What limited assistance would enable completion?

Prioritize follow-up using supply criticality, switching difficulty, likely emissions relevance, spending, and readiness. Geography can help coordinate visits; it should not be the sole selection rule. Follow up on nonresponse before inferring incapability.

## 2. Match support to the obstacle

| Response | Support |
|---|---|
| Suitable reporting exists | Email request, permission to share, desk review |
| Records exist but format is unclear | Bilingual instructions and templates |
| Specific records/calculations are difficult | Time-limited individual specialist help |
| Significant gaps at a critical supplier | Named Merit relationship owner; specialist assistance and targeted site visit where justified |

**Verification is a separate dimension:** a coach helps prepare information; a reviewer checks it; independent assurance requires a separate qualified engagement. Do not classify every low-readiness supplier as needing an audit.

Supplier owns and approves its information. Merit owns request design, procurement relationships, data acceptance, and integration into its inventory.

## 3. Progress through evidence levels

| Level | Required result |
|---|---|
| Reported | A supplier gives a figure; method/evidence may be missing |
| Traceable initial data | Period, boundary, source records, calculation method, factors, assumptions, and gaps are documented |
| Robust repeatable reporting | Reviewed annual records, defensible allocation to Merit, relevant upstream coverage, and a process the supplier can repeat |

Merit can begin estimates from purchasing records before receiving full supplier inventories. The [data note](../research/supplier-emissions-data-and-ecovadis.md) explains the minimum and the upgrade path.

## 4. Support suppliers without creating dependency

Accept reusable evidence; avoid duplicate questionnaires; give a clear explanation of the business need. Offer individual help in the supplier's language when necessary.

Consider assessment subsidies or commercial incentives only after diagnosing the obstacle. Longer contracts/preferred status require normal quality, delivery, commercial, and procurement review. Do not guarantee business or reward artificially low emissions.

Government resources are supplementary. No IFC partnership, government-funded reporting cohort, or automatic NAFIN ESG discount has been confirmed.

## 5. Economics and timing

The old approximately $73,000 estimate covered a 12-supplier intensive cohort, not one supplier. It included shared preparation/training costs and is **not the current budget**.

Use a capped support fund with case-by-case authorization; obtain quotes after initial readiness information. [Budget and decision log](../research/supplier-support-budget-and-decisions.md).

Proposed targets for assisted suppliers, subject to records and scope: a first reviewed pack in 4–8 weeks after the individual work begins; stronger annual documentation over 6–12 months. These are planning assumptions, not measured industry averages. Missing historical records may require a full collection year plus review.

## 6. Values, risk, and measurement

- **Health:** preserve supply continuity; do not claim reporting alone improves patient or worker outcomes.
- **Excellence:** consistent evidence and calculation acceptance criteria.
- **Agility:** different assistance for different barriers.
- **Responsibility:** honest coverage, gaps, estimates, and annual updates.
- **Teamwork:** listen, minimize burden, and help suppliers sustain the process.

Track accepted data coverage by spend and critical suppliers; assistance required; cost and hours per accepted submission; remaining estimates; supplier burden; and ability to repeat the process. Keep questionnaire completion, quantitative data quality, and independent assurance separate.

The [adjusted rubric](../case-materials/Judging%20Sheet.txt) gives social impact 25%. Measure supplier capability and reporting burden; do not substitute attendance or paperwork volume for impact.

## Executive storyline

1. We need visibility across the supplier base without weakening critical relationships.
2. Request and reuse existing information first.
3. Diagnose gaps and assign the least intensive effective support.
4. Fund targeted help and conditional incentives.
5. Accept traceable data, improve it over time, and report limitations honestly.
6. Expand spending only when demonstrated needs and results justify it.
